---
title: The afterword of a book made me cry
---

I completed reading _'Tuesdays with Moorie'_ yesterday. 

I put a status update on my  WhatsApp close friends and now my Instagram feed is all about that book. But that's not what I want to sulk about right now. I am not sure yet on how I am going to act out on the overwhelmingness of reading something of that sort. I am in awe of the courage and will Mitch might've had to gather to be able to revisit his Tuesdays with Moorie and write about it. To have listened to those recorded tapes. For the kind of person I am and the values/beliefs that I lead my everyday life with, the concepts put forth by Moorie aren't really "life altering" or 'unlocking a new perspective" for me as it were for Mitch or any other typical reader. 

The afterword of the book is what got to me. Mitch doesn't really talk a lot about his experience of the Tuesdays, most of it is just plain descriptions and requotes of what Moorie said and how Mitch felt being around him. So maybe it is unfair for me come at myself for not being able to articulate my experience of it too. But Mitch's ability to reproduce that experience for his readers is an something -- to talk about. In the afterword, Mitch talks of how Moorie never read a word of the book and yet so many of us are a part of this big classroom of his that he never entered yet managed to touch so many. It speaks volumes of how less aware we are (especially quantitively) of the scope and reaches of love and kindness. And so much of our rationality is based on the pieces which very often misses this piece. That explains why the line "Love is the only rational act" stands out of it all so much, for everyone who has ever read the book.

I really hope someone from Mitch's life decides to write about his experience. I wonder what questions came to Mitch when he was sitting next to Moorie's bed, what all crossed his mind when he used to drive to Moorie's every Tuesday. Has he ever felt the guilt of not asking questions in the 16 years that passed? If so, does he still feel it or he now knows that there iwas't another way for it to be. I want to know the details of how Moorie spoke to him from his grave and help him with all he has.

Living by the "dramatic" values that I do, with a bird on my shoulder whispering "Is today the day I die?", I am aware that there's always room to be a bit more sensitive to that whisper than I already am. And I love how I know that despite my above average sensitivity to that whisper, when I am on my death bed, I'd have my share of learnings just like Moorie did, despite living a reasonably above average fulfilling life. And that makes me nothing but more excited for the days to come.

I truly do believe that the book called me to pick it up this time, on my way to my hometown. Given to me by a very dear professor of mine, Ankita Ma'am, I've had this book around for a while. I am super grateful to be a part of her dear classroom, that extends beyond the walls of my college. Moorie, in his ways, for Mitch has been someone who always taught him how to be more human, and you, in all ways, always will be an epitome of ways to be a better more fierce woman. 

Thank you for all your kindness dear Ankita Ma'am.
