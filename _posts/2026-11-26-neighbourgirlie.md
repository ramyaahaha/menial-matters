title: I am friends with my neighbour
---
_Neighbour friends, ritualistic friendships, and the luxury of not having to have a plan._

I recently became friends with my neighbour. She's a year younger to me. An elder sibling. I've known about her existence for over three years now. 

This is someone who was very much a stranger, but not as much as a passerby stranger - but a regular one.

The kind you see in the elevator often enough to recognise their face, know which floor they get off at, and have a vague idea of their daily routine. The kind you see often enough to know that you speak the same language at home. You know their face, but not their name. You know which door they walk into, but not what goes on behind it. You might recognise their footsteps in the corridor, even notice that they haven't been around for a few days, but wouldn't really know whether to ask about it or not.

There is a strange familiarity in repeatedly seeing someone without ever actually meeting them.

She, I believe, is the most textbook definition of a “stranger” a person has been to me. We'd look at the ceiling or the elevator buttons rather than say "Hi", because we don't do that anymore. For years, we had managed to remain strangers despite living next to each other. Familiar with each other's existence, but not acquainted with each other's lives.

And I think that was a little unfortunate. Not because I think everyone needs to be friends with everyone, or because I am particularly fond of making small talk in elevators. But because I think these things matter. I'd much rather have my worries occupied by the larger, more consequential societal concerns than by the awkwardness of saying hello to someone who lives ten feet away from me. I'd like for these little interactions to become normal and ordinary again.

How many times does someone have to appear in your everyday life before they stop being a stranger? Does familiarity come from “knowing” someone, or simply from having seen them around enough times? I think it’s the later.

Anyway, we're friends now. Spontaneously. 

And I think I missed having these kinds of friends more than I had realised.

My building friends, I miss the theme of evenings I associate with them. The evenings when we did not necessarily know what we’d end up doing. We just knew we had to spend time together, as a ritual.  They know things about me only because they happened to be around when those things happened. Silly embarrassing things, phases of life that I probably wouldn't voluntarily narrate to anyone today. Some of them now, despite years of barely any contact and keeping up are my closest friends.

I wonder if we became close because we were compatible people as friends, or if we became compatible by being constantly around each other.

There's a certain ‘time-to-leave’ feeling associated with when I meet my non-neighbour friends.

Decided to meet someone? Decide on a place, travel there, spend a few hours together, and somewhere in the back of my mind I am already calculating when I should be leaving. There's an invisible fucking expiry time clock that comes with it. And that steals something from the experience. And the next time I meet them is one long plan away.

Pragnya and I we don't have particularly meaningful conversations or shared interests/subjects yet. But she does know how I look when I am bored, loitering around in the house when I am alone and there's absolutely nothing happening. I can walk to her house without putting on any footwear, borrow sugar or curd if we ran out. Exchange Wi-Fi. There was no need to make an occasion out of any of this. It’s just stupidly ordinary things one would do with the person next door.

--

We don't have to make the most of an evening because there will be other evenings; till she moves out. And yet, there's a certain luxury in this kind of availability. And I think I had forgotten what it felt like. There's something quite lovely about having someone available for the mundane parts of your life. Random Tuesday evenings. The days when you don't particularly want to be alone, but also don't have anything specific to do or say.

Someone who knows the tiny little details and events of my day. The things that don't really make it past the bar of being relevant or important enough to be brought up during my monthly meet-up with friends over drinks. From  the random thing my classmate said about the news to I spilled milk on the gas stove. These aren't necessarily things I’d keep a track of or remember to tell my friends when I meet them after a month; but they're still things that happened and it's nice to have someone who knows about them.
