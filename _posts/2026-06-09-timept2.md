•	The "time passes through us" vs "we pass through time" distinction

•	Randomness in terms of change - What we call randomness may reflect hidden structure that we do not yet understand.

•	Vectors points – 3b1b

•	Clock time versus lived time

•	Clock time transforms vectors into points. Human experience transforms points back into vectors.

•	If everything changes, why is our metric of change not subject to change?

•	Feeling of towards. - Teleology, Phenomenology

•	Entropy

•	“Timeless means changeless.” Is not right - Change is fundamental, and the experience of time is how we make sense of it. 

•	Explains why time is always moving forward. It’s oriented, like everything else.

•	The issue may not be clock time itself. The issue may be allowing clock time to become the only way we understand existence. What aspects of human life become distorted when clock time becomes the dominant lens through which we understand ourselves?

•	Not time as quantity. Not time as natural cycle. But time as orientation. - What if people don't fundamentally live by clocks or cycles? What if they live by attractions (ref:seductions)?

•	Perhaps the primary structure of human experience is not temporal but directional.

•	Change <-> Time <-> randomeness and their relation so as to is one more fundamental than the other? How can their relationship be best visualized?

•	Is orientation more fundamental than time, or is orientation only possible because time exists?
