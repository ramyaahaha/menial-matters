---
title: Perpendiculars as the geometry of seduction 
---

**Perpendicular** – lines intersecting at an angle of 90°. Also, the the shortest distance from a 
point to a line. 

Before you begin, I'd like to request a small concession from geometry.  

Throughout this essay, I use "parallel" and "perpendicular" in ways not limited to mean lines separated by a fixed distance, or lines meeting at exactly 90°. I intend to use them more as tendencies than measurements. 

A parallel is anything that continues an existing direction.
A perpendicular is anything that introduces a new one. A perspective.

I ask that you see “lines” merely just not as objects drawn on paper, but as trajectories of thought, experience, meaning, and consciousness itself. 

---

In mathematics, physics, and general study of life and experiences (particularly cognition if I may), perpendicular components often represent independent degrees of freedom, which can 
enrich a system's description and hence its understanding. 

If seduction manifests as possibility, then possibility itself may be understood geometrically through perpendiculars. This is what makes perpendicular lines feel more informative and interesting than parallel lines to me. A parallel perspective extends existing information and a perpendicular perspective 
introduces a new one.  Parallel lines preserve identity. Perpendicular lines create possibility. That is a view; a perspective. 

Identity is continuity and possibility is transformation. And the best most happy life seems to require both. Too much parallelity and nothing changes. Too much perpendicularity and nothing remains. Maybe meaning exists and in fact emerges from the tension between them. 

---

**Perpendiculars and interruption.**

Parallel lines continue. Perpendicular lines interrupt. 
A thought interrupts. A question interrupts. A kiss interrupts. A heartbreak interrupts. Death interrupts. A revelation interrupts. 
Every meaningful event in life arrives from and exists outside the trajectory we are already on. A parallel line seems to tell me where I am going and a perpendicular line tells me about all 
elsewhere I can be. 

Seduction, I believe can also very well be described as the experience of being interrupted. Often elegantly, in the greater grander scheme of life. 

A moment that interrupts, a breath that interrupts, a person that interrupts, an experience that interrupts. When somebody seduces you, they don't extend your existing reality. They break into it.
They introduce another axis. Another possibility. Another future. And at times, very gracefully, another (view of the) past. Suddenly somehow the old trajectory isn't complete enough.  That's why certain people become irresistible. Not because they agree with us, not because they're opposite to who and what we are but because they're orthogonal to us. 

Opposites define each other; orthogonals reveal each other. Opposites compete for position; orthogonals create dimension. Opposites ask us to choose; orthogonals ask us to expand. 

Maybe that’s what love is. Do we fall in love with those who mirror us? Or with those who allow us to reveal dimensions of our being we couldn’t ever access alone. 

---

**Perpendiculars as tools to access science better.**

The realisation of perpendicularity is what enables us to understand and make sense of complexity through independent dimensions. Much of mathematics, physics, and engineering begins by introducing new dimensions rather 
than extending existing ones. We resolve physical forces into perpendicular components to understand them independently and re-combine them because complexity becomes 
manageable when viewed from and across multiple directions. Light itself cannot be entirely described by a single component, but by the interaction and coexistence of multiple independent ones. A wave propagates because multiple 
perpendicular aspects coexist and interact. 

In calculus and in experience, the most revealing quantities are often not of the object itself but what changes around it and because of it. Meta data of the process exists in gradients and 
normals; these are the tools to access information hidden within the original structure of a function. 

Modern physics answers questions better, and has raised better questions about our understanding of the universe because it fundamentally works by introducing new dimensions to existing understanding and available information.  

Our number system too is wonderful example of the same. It’s beautiful almost magical how complex numbers allow us to witness and understand the world better mathematically, when real numbers weren’t enough to describe what we saw. This particular thought is heavily inspired from and aided by all of what and how Grant Sanderson speaks of in his lecture.

**“Gods” and their access to perpendiculars**

Across religions and cultures, Gods or any other superior beings are rarely portrayed as merely faster or stronger than us humans. They're believed to be beings who can see, perceive and 
understand what humans cannot. They’re said to perceive - the future, hidden motives and intentions, not very obvious and often surprising interconnected consequences and multiple 
truths simultaneously.  

What enables them to do so? 

Maybe they inhabit dimensions unavailable to us. Perhaps what we call intelligence is not the accumulation of knowledge, but the discovery of new perpendiculars. 

---

**Perpendiculars, intelligence and empathy**

Opposites or parallels fundamentally demand choice. If I see your position as the opposite of mine, one of us must be wrong. But if I see your position as another axis, then your experience may contain information that my axis does not capture at all. 

Orthogonals coexist and jointly produce a richer model of reality. 

Treating happiness and sadness as opposites of each other, requires one to eliminate the other. But if they were treated as orthogonals, they each reveal something the other cannot. And that explains how and why we experience the blend of all opposites; and why life exists as a balance. Most and infact seemingly all conflicts happen because we try to force reality into smaller dimensions. Often, very naively just a line.

Good ↔ Bad, Success ↔ Failure, Happy ↔ Sad, Left ↔ Right, Love ↔ Hate, Life ↔ Death 

In this single line perspective, everything has to fit into buckets of opposites. But that’s rarely how we experience things, emotions and people. Life ceases to exist in this single dimension. 
Introduction of axes eliminates this contradiction and allows empathy. An unempathetic interpretation of another person is placing them on your axis. Hence, you see the world as you are, or only as you can. What may seem like contradiction in one dimension maybe actually coherence in two and so on. 

The perpendicular may be the simplest visual expression of intelligence itself: the capacity to accommodate a new dimension without collapsing the existing one. Maybe that’s what intelligence is at the abstract level when we speak of consciousness. The ability to perceive and operate across more dimensions than one can. We’re taught to see right vs wrong, good vs bad, winning vs losing etc and so on. And we grow to learn that good intentions can cause harm, loss often results in growth, and happiness can only coexist with grief and not otherwise. Maturity and growing up is adding of these dimensions to our lens that we perceive through. 
And slowly, the world doesn’t seem that complicated anymore, not because it has changed, but because it becomes less reducible to a single line in our heads. Introduction of those new dimensions equips us to better understand, break down, deal with, experience and manage the complexities of this world. 

---

A point can only know itself. A line can understand positions along itself. A plane can see the entirety of a line, but not of depth and so on. It’s not the limitation of the human brain but in the behaviour of geometry of this world that we live in to push us beyond what we can see, to enable to always experience more.

Perpendicularity manifests as the possibility of being more than one thing at once. And that is why it best represents the geometry of seduction.
