---
title: Board game nights @varun's home
---

Over the last couple of months, my a very dear friend of mine, Varun has been kind enough to host board game nights at his place; he also does a really good job being effortlessly persistent about it. Despite being a corporate guy, he manages to fit us in. He is who I'd say is someone who has mastered the skill of building a community. A community that often extends beyond him.
He's had it in him since he was a kid. Someone who's really into making things happen. From a trip across states to let's please watch Verity. 

Being around Varun, teaches you specifically, how to be a better friend - maybe often not to him, but to your other friends and friend groups. And that's why I say, he contributes to building communities that expand far beyond his reach. 

Last year, when I was in office in Goregaon, Varun was at home playing video games I believe. I called him up to go to my place and send me a snip of something I had written in my notepad. There were three notepads on my table, and I couldn't figure out on video call which was the one. So Varun, travelled a 1.5 hours from BARC to my office, in his shorts to hand me my notepads before the meeting because I couldn't figure out my own handwriting on a video call. I still think about that very often, and what it does to me know that I have a fire fighter fire extinguisher friend. He helplessly teaches you how to be a friend, by very simply being one.

I cannot help but dismiss all the instagram reels asking you to draw strict boundaries, quantify the give and takes, to prioritise your convenience over everything else in a dynamic. It's stupid. Because if it weren't for him taking up the inconvenience of creating a WhatsApp group, constantly tagging us all to show up to play a board game and also clean up his house before we showed up, none of the 9-10 of us would have had a third place; and I would've never grown fond of a bunch of AECS-4 people talking about climate change and being high in college classrooms. I am now also fond of multiple video games and I see why there's a community around it too.

There isn't a lot to write about the experience of playing board games because of how ordinary, average and frequently back home it feels to be there. I hope we really continue to sustain this space that we have, with a fair share of just whiling away time. 

Thank you dear, for everything.

I wonder if he'd be able to get us all to binge watch off campus with him before this year ends. Well, we'll find out.
